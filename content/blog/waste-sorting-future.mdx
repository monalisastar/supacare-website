### Introduction

Urban streets across rapidly growing cities like Nairobi, Lagos, and Jakarta are struggling with **overflowing bins, illegal dumpsites, and clogged drainage systems**.

The truth is simple: **urban cleanliness begins at the source**.

Sorting waste where it originates—in homes, offices, and institutions—is no longer optional; it’s a **strategic necessity** for healthier, more sustainable cities.

---

### What Is Source Waste Sorting?

Source sorting is the process of separating waste into categories *before* it leaves your doorstep:

- 🟢 **Organics** – food scraps, garden waste  
- 🔵 **Recyclables** – plastics, paper, metal  
- ⚫ **Residuals** – diapers, contaminated plastics  

This small change at the household level produces massive downstream benefits: cleaner streets, lower collection costs, and empowered citizens.

### Why Cities Need Source Sorting

Urbanization brings concrete, congestion—and **mountains of unsegregated waste**.

When households mix organics, plastics, and residuals:

- Recyclables become contaminated and useless  
- Organic waste rots, emitting methane  
- Waste workers face hazardous conditions  
- Collection and disposal become inefficient  

Source sorting prevents these issues and promotes **efficient waste management from the start**.

---

### Real-World Impact: Cleaner, Smarter Cities

Cities that enforce or promote source sorting report:

- ✅ **Reduced landfill overflow**  
- ✅ **Lower municipal collection costs**  
- ✅ **Cleaner markets, estates, and public spaces**  
- ✅ **New green jobs in recycling and composting**  
- ✅ **Empowered and responsible households**

### How Families and Communities Can Participate

You don’t need to wait for laws. Start with small actions:

- Introduce **separate bins** in your home or apartment block  
- Teach children proper sorting techniques  
- Support community waste programs  
- Choose waste service providers that support smart sorting  

The goal is **progress, not perfection**.

### Quick Tips for Effective Waste Sorting

1. Separate **organics, recyclables, and residuals** at the point of disposal  
2. Keep bins clean and labeled  
3. Encourage family or neighbors to participate  
4. Compost organics when possible  
5. Support local recycling initiatives
