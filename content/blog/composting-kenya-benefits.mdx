### Introduction

In the corners of kitchens, gardens, and school compounds across Kenya, something powerful is happening.

What was once "waste" is being turned into **living, breathing soil**.

**Composting**—the age-old practice of transforming organic matter into rich fertilizer—is experiencing a bold revival in urban and rural areas alike.

And it's more than just a trend. It’s a movement rooted in **sustainability, education, and community empowerment**.

---

### What Exactly Is Composting?

Composting is the controlled decomposition of organic waste—think fruit peels, maize husks, vegetable scraps, and dry leaves—into nutrient-rich humus.

This process:
- Converts waste into value
- Reduces pressure on landfills
- Eliminates methane emissions from rot
- Produces natural fertilizer for crops and gardens

### Why Kenya Needs Composting

Urbanization has brought concrete, congestion—and **mountains of food waste**.

Studies show that **over 50% of household waste in Kenyan cities is organic**. But instead of being returned to the soil, it’s dumped in Dandora, Ruai, and countless illegal sites.

Composting provides a solution that is:
- ✅ Low-cost
- ✅ Community-friendly
- ✅ Educational
- ✅ Scalable from balconies to farms

### Composting in Schools: A Living Lab

At Mwihoko High School in Kiambu, SupaCare helped set up a **3-bin compost system**.

Within 8 weeks:
- Students composted over **200 kg** of food scraps
- The school's kitchen garden was revived
- Science teachers used the bins to teach decomposition, biology, and environmental studies
- Students took the practice **home to their families**

<BlogImage src="/images/blogs/composting-kenya-benefits/impact.png" alt="Lush vegetables growing from school compost" />

The result? A new generation of climate-conscious citizens—starting with a shovel and a few food scraps.

### How Families Are Joining the Movement

In apartment blocks and gated homes, composting is going mini:

- **Plastic drums** with air holes are turning into DIY composters
- **Bokashi systems** work well indoors without smell
- **Worm farms** (vermiculture) produce compost tea for potted plants

Even a small household can compost **3–5 kg of waste per week**—diverting 150+ kg per year from landfills.

### Quick Tips for Starting Composting

1. Separate “greens” (fruit, veg scraps) and “browns” (dry leaves, paper)
2. Layer them like a lasagna (greens, browns, greens…)
3. Keep the pile **moist but not wet**
4. Turn weekly to add oxygen
5. After 6–8 weeks, enjoy your dark, earthy compost
